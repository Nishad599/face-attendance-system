Privacy Notice

How this attendance system uses your face data.

In short: we convert your face into a numeric code so a camera can mark you present. We never share it, you can withdraw at any time, and withdrawing deletes it.

What we collect

  • Face data — photographs taken during registration, and a mathematical representation (an "encoding") derived from them. The encoding cannot be turned back into a photograph of you.
  • Your details — name, roll number, email, date of birth, batch.
  • Attendance records — the date, time and session you were marked for.

Why we collect it

Solely to record attendance for your course, and to produce the attendance reports your institute requires. It is not used for any other purpose, and it is not used to track your location or behaviour outside marking attendance.

Who can see it

Your teachers and the institute administrators. Your face data is never sold, never shared with third parties, and never leaves the institute's own server — this system does not send your data to any external or cloud service.

How long we keep it

Your face data is deleted within {{ retention_days }} days of your course ending. Attendance records are kept for longer, because they form part of your academic record — but those contain no biometric information.

Your rights

  • Withdraw consent at any time, from your student portal. Your face data is deleted immediately and the camera will stop recognising you. Your teacher will mark you manually instead — you will not be disadvantaged, and your attendance history is unaffected.
  • Ask what we hold about you, and have mistakes corrected — use "My details" in your portal, or ask your teacher.
  • Decline entirely. Face registration is not compulsory. If you prefer not to give consent, tell your teacher and you will be marked manually.

Keeping it safe

The system runs on the institute's own server over an encrypted connection. Access requires a login, every account is role-restricted, and changes to attendance records are logged.

Questions

Speak to your teacher or the institute administrator. If you believe your data has been mishandled, you may raise it with them or with the Data Protection Board of India.
